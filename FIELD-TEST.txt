PAGERWATCH 1.0.11 — FIELD CHECK

1. Start: check Field reliability and confirm active capture interface.
   Receiving observations confirms reception. Quiet RF alone is not failure.
2. USB: plug in, unplug, and replug your supported external adapter.
   Confirm the source switches and the dashboard remains reachable.
3. Manual fallback: pin the external adapter, unplug it and confirm a retry
   is reported. Return to Auto to enable Pager fallback.
4. Phone connection: briefly disconnect Wi-Fi, then reconnect. Confirm the
   snapshot warning clears. Keep GPS sharing open and verify a NEW fix.
5. GPS: temporarily deny/disable location. Confirm stale readings stop being
   used; re-enable permission and share again. Check the GPS-gap counter.
6. Interrupted run: use a disposable test session. After a checkpoint and
   some sightings, interrupt the payload process. Relaunch, then review the
   prior run in Sessions. Verify complete sightings and the recovery notice.
   Do not deliberately cut power to a card holding your only copy of logs.
7. Storage: check free space and journal usage. Use a disposable test setup
   to reach the configured log limit. Confirm the warning stays visible.
8. Load: compare response and queue drops with a crowded device list. Export
   diagnostics if the UI stalls; memory/CPU and recovery events are included.
9. Update: preserve config.json and .state. Copy the complete release. If a
   mixed-file warning appears, replace all release files, then relaunch.

10. Known gear: mark your own address, toggle Hide known devices, and verify
    it disappears from live radar/lists. Choose Known devices in inventory to
    find it again. Check Watchlist > Known devices after it leaves the live
    inventory; unmarking must preserve any label/bookmark. Confirm exports
    still include the address. Privacy must mask known-device details.
11. Compare: finish two short runs with one shared address and a new address.
    Compare them in Sessions. Check new/returning/not-seen lists, Privacy
    masking and the partial-inventory warning when a run has evictions.
12. Signal: track a device with changing RSSI. Check latest vs average, then
    stop its transmissions. Average/live sound must expire after ten seconds.
13. GPS coverage: record before sharing GPS, with a fresh fix, then with a
    stale fix. Counts must sum to accepted observations. Review/export the
    finished run. Low-accuracy fixes should be counted if your phone supplies
    them; do not spoof coordinates in a run you intend to keep.
14. Cleanup: create disposable runs, select one, review size, cancel, then
    select again and confirm permanent deletion. Check files are gone and
    the active run cannot be selected/deleted. Do not use your only logs.

15. Native menus: without using the dashboard controls, open FIELD and scroll
    through all nine options. Check run summary, GPS status and return with B.
    Phone GPS still requires the phone to share its location.
16. Device actions: A in details opens actions; mark known, bookmark and toggle
    an address watch. Verify these changes appear in the dashboard and remain
    after relaunch. Confirm Right still tracks and exclusion can be cancelled.
17. Native Privacy: enable it, then view GPS, known gear, saved inventory and
    comparisons. Identifiers/coordinates/movement must be masked; address
    editing is blocked until Privacy is off.
18. Native saved runs: browse pages, review a run, compare vs current and
    change categories. Cancel a loading view: it must not reopen on completion.
    On disposable data only, preview/cancel deletion, then confirm another
    deletion. The active run must remain protected. Capture should continue.

19. Research: open a device in the dashboard. Inspect matched conditions and
    source references. Save notes/links, attach a small JPEG/PNG, reload, and
    verify both persist. Concurrent stale saves should report a conflict.
20. History: finish two test runs with a shared address, then research that
    address. Verify times, source-count labels and receiver GPS locations.
    Check older pages and partial-read warnings. Rotated addresses stay apart.
21. Research Privacy: open photos/notes/history, enable Privacy and confirm
    sensitive details disappear. Turn it off and check your unsaved draft is
    retained. On the Pager, research details must also stay masked.
22. Evidence: export the device ZIP. Open report.html on your Windows PC;
    inspect JSON, photos and source links. Verify SHA256SUMS if needed.
    Exports retain originals and contain the selected history page only.
23. Native research: open Device actions > Research / past sightings. Scroll
    notes/evidence and page through history. B must return correctly; cancel
    a pending read and ensure it does not reopen after you leave the screen.

Record Pager firmware, adapter model/driver, active interface and test result.
Host tests simulate faults; this checklist validates behavior on your hardware.

RECON CLEANUP CHECK — 1.0.11
- Confirm Handshakes and deauth controls are absent from the dashboard and
  Pager device menus. Device actions should wrap correctly through six items.
- Launch after an overwrite update. The retired module should be removed;
  config.json, .state and all saved session folders must stay intact.
- Leave your supported USB adapter plugged in at startup. In Auto, verify it
  becomes the receiver without repeated replugs. Unplug and confirm fallback
  to Pager Wi-Fi. Replug and check automatic selection; try manual fallback.
- Open Devices, radar, coverage, Phone GPS, research and Sessions. Confirm
  current observations, Privacy, receiver health and exports still work.
- Recheck GPS coordinates, speed, heading, fresh-fix tagging and recovery after
  a brief phone disconnect. Quiet RF must not trigger receiver resets.
- Download diagnostics after any remaining error and note the active adapter.

MAC DETECTION CHECK — 1.0.11
- Compare the exact observed MAC and Wi-Fi/Bluetooth type with your other
  detector. Find the same address in PagerWatch Devices, not only Alerts.
- A B4:1E:52 Wi-Fi transmitter or public Bluetooth address should show the
  Flock Safety radio hint, even with no name. Default alert threshold is 75;
  a higher saved threshold or known/excluded device settings may suppress it.
- Random/unknown Bluetooth addresses must not gain a vendor from their MAC.
- Test 2.4 GHz mode near the camera and check active receiver health. Missing
  reception is separate from a received address with no signature match.
- If still missed, save diagnostics and record the other detector's MAC,
  protocol, channel and timestamp. Do not assume a shared chipset is Flock.

WARDRIVE PROBE CHECK — 1.0.11
- Near your local camera, compare its exact MAC in Wardrive Go and PagerWatch.
  Try 2.4 GHz mode and confirm the active receiver is receiving traffic.
- For a listed shared prefix sending a wildcard probe, expect Possible Flock
  wildcard probe, not a confirmed camera/model label. Default threshold is 75.
- A directed probe, beacon or missing SSID must not activate that wildcard
  candidate rule. Ordinary devices may also send matching wildcard probes.
- If still missed, export diagnostics and capture the MAC/protocol/channel
  reported by the other detector. Reception and classification are separate.

RADAR / TRACKER CHECK — 1.0.11
- Compare the exact radio address and protocol against another detector.
- Radar defaults to Session contacts. Wait over 30 seconds without receiving
  a contact: it should remain dimmed. Test Live (30s) and Recent (5m) filters.
  Unknown RSSI should appear hollow at the edge, not as an invented strength.
- Present your own Tile and AirTag separately. Check scanner metadata counts,
  Device signature evidence, and Alerts. AirTags may use different formats
  depending on state; quiet/connected devices may not emit a matching record.
- A later name-only packet must not erase prior evidence or generate repeated
  alerts for one exact address/session. Verify last-observed signature times.
- Check default threshold 75 and filters: a higher threshold, exclusions or
  Hide known devices may suppress feedback/display. Quiet profile suppresses
  sound/vibration; it does not remove recorded matches.
- For Flock, inspect the exact MAC and observed signature evidence. A shared
  wildcard-probe candidate is not a confirmed camera. Try 2.4 GHz coverage.
- If detection fails, export Controls & exports > Build diagnostic report
  immediately while the comparison detector still sees the target. Record
  the target MAC/protocol, Pager firmware and USB adapter, plus a screenshot
  of the affected live view. Redact location data if sharing publicly.

1.0.11 RELEASE GATE — RECORD ACTUAL RESULTS
Use FIELD-TEST-RESULTS.csv. Host simulations are not hardware verification.
1. Present your own Tile, AirTag and local Flock radio separately. Record the
   exact address/protocol, comparison detector, channel and time. Run Alerts >
   Why wasn't this detected? Compare receipt, metadata, signature and decision.
2. Trigger the same signature repeatedly. Expect one alert per exact address
   and protocol/session; confirm Quiet and Hide known behavior separately.
3. Leave Bluetooth scanning for at least 30 minutes. Watch recent report and
   metadata ages. Quiet RF must not trigger repeated scanner changes. Export
   diagnostics immediately if names keep arriving while metadata ages grow.
4. Start with USB, unplug, confirm Pager fallback, replug, confirm USB choice.
   Repeat five times. Confirm phone dashboard/AP remains usable throughout.
5. Run a 60-minute drive with phone GPS shared. Check scanner ages, queue drops,
   memory, recording health, session contacts after 30s, and exported sightings.
6. Turn Privacy on while a detection check is pending: no target details should
   remain visible. Downloaded diagnostics intentionally contain original data.
7. Export diagnostics after any failure, noting time, firmware and adapter.
   Save the ZIP alongside this result sheet; do not publish private identifiers.

1.0.11 RESPONSIVENESS CHECK
- Run at least 20 minutes in the same busy RF environment as the lag report.
- Test rapid short UP/DOWN taps, tab switching, device actions and Back.
- Repeat with the phone dashboard closed, then open. Note which tab is active.
- Confirm checkpoints complete without recurring button freezes. Verify saved
  inventories and journal updates after a normal exit and an interrupted run.
- Confirm individual signature alerts remain recorded during a burst; physical
  vibration is intentionally limited to one pulse per two seconds.
- Check RSSI updates and detection when a new BLE service/manufacturer payload
  appears between repeated advertisements. The raw receive counters count all
  reports; redundant session emissions can be combined for up to 500 ms.
- If lag returns, note duration/adapter/tab/dashboard connection, longest control
  loop gap, slow turns, queue drops, memory and CPU. Export diagnostics before
  restarting. Host timing results do not establish actual Pager input latency.
